Hyperion has been on my TBR list for a while and I’m glad I finally got to read it. The book is a collection of seven short stories revolving around a group of pilgrims going to the enigmatic Time Tombs on the outskirts planet of Hyperion.

Each tale is told in turn by each pilgrim. They all have their own compelling reasons for going and each tale is told in a different genre. For example an action packed thriller leads into a film noir detective who-done-it which leads into a heartbreaking father-daughter story. Admittedly I might not have been in the right frame of mind when I started this book. The first couple tales start out slowly so it wasn’t until the 3rd that I was hooked. The father-daughter story especially got me emotional as it reminded me of the relationship with my own 3 year old son. I could tell the author has kids of his own as it felt all too real at times. At times in this tale it was like watching someone die, but “backwards.” A feeling as heartbreaking as it was novel.

Each story ultimately revolves around Hyperion and the Shrike at the heart of each story. It was remarkable how Dan Simmons was able to weave so many varied stories around such an enigmatic being. My theory as to how he did it is _because_ the Shrike is so mysterious and so unknowable. They can pop in and out of anywhere at any time. They can be whatever the author needs them to be. Perhaps they are the same for each character? Which makes the bullshit ending that much more jarring. Instead of resolving these character conflicts that we spend an entire book laying out, it ends just before they reach the Time Tombs.

It was a great book and I enjoyed my time with it. In fairness, Dan Simmons had to chop Hyperion into 2 books due to length and I hear that the sequel is just as good and resolves all the plot threads so I’ll be putting that book on my TBR list as well. I badly need to see how this ends.
