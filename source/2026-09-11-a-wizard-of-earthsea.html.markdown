This book has instantly become a top five of all time for me. I knew within the first couple pages that this was something special. And I’m not alone. This has been a loved book since its release in the 1960s. I find myself at a loss to describe this book in a way that does it justice. To start, the prose is achingly beautiful.

The pictures conjured in my mind from Le Guin’s prose are far more than what the words actually say. There’s an indescribable poetic feeling to Le Guin’s scenes and images. I actually felt like I was tossed on the sea with the protagonist. I sat in rapt awe at the dragon that unfurled itself from the ruined tower and loomed over the town. I loved that scene. It was one of several scenes that made me feel a genuine sense of awe and terror in a way that I haven’t felt in a long time. I actually wanted to physically cower as I read it. I reread that scene again while writing this review and it still awed me. A sure sign that it wasn’t just me being in the right mindset to be wowed, but because the book did it so well.

I was enthralled from the start because Le Guin executes at such a high level and so consistently. That execution built a trust in the book to nail the next anticipated scene. This contrasted so strongly with the last book I read _The Aeronaut's Windlass_. A quarter of the way into that one and I knew the author wasn’t going to pull it off, so I lost interest. This is a new theory on story craft I’m concocting.


The magic system is based on knowing a beings “true” name. Everything and everyone has a public or common name, and an inner and private “true” name. The “true” name has power, power the magically adept can use or abuse. It’s a beautiful system for the ethereal metaphorical style Le Guin employs. The magic system also deals heavily with maintaining balance in a taoist yin and yang manner. Every use of magic has to be weighed carefully so as not to disrupt that delicate balance. 

The world in _A Wizard of Earthsea_ is a collection of island archipelagos amongst an endless ocean. The races and characters that inhabit these islands are all distinct making the world feel alive. The legends these peoples hold tell of a creation myth of a primordial being who drove the mountains out of the endless sea. I love how these little bits of lore leave me yearning for more. Is the sea really endless? Was it always so endless? We are told dragons are older than the race of men, how did they become? How did men?


The protagonist is a young boy named Ged whom we are told in the very beginning will eventually become the strongest wizard in the history of Earthsea. But right now he is an arrogant and prideful little boy with more power than he thinks he knows how to handle. He also doesn’t understand the need for balance and he ends up paying for it. We then watch him grow into a wiser young man who learns to carefully consider the consequences of his actions before making them. A lesson I’m sure every parent wishes their kid would learn. It is my theory that the primordial being was actually a wizard who broke the balance of the world and turned it into an endless sea, and then tried to fix it.
